Gaze-Aware Human-Robot Interaction

I develop low-cost, calibration-free gaze systems that enable social robots to infer human attention and autonomously manage interaction flow in real-world settings.

Selected Papers System Demos

Research Contributions

  • Demonstrated that appearance-based gaze estimation models reliably capture human attention patterns in live human-robot interaction.
  • Identified tablet-based interaction as a dominant attentional attractor that can override task relevance in multimodal HRI.
  • Developed a real-time gaze-based mechanism for detecting task completion without explicit user input.
  • Created annotation pipelines and benchmarks supporting reproducible gaze research in HRI.

Selected Systems and Demos

Gaze-Based Task Completion Detection (GARI)

A real-time system enabling Pepper to autonomously advance tablet-based interactions using natural gaze shifts as completion signals.

Multimodal Gaze Tracking in Live HRI

A controlled incongruence experiment combining eye-tracking glasses and appearance-based gaze models to analyze attention across modalities.

Automatic Gaze Target Annotation

A pipeline for automatically identifying gaze targets in static HRI scenes, reducing manual annotation effort for engagement analysis.

About

I am a PhD candidate at Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam working on gaze-aware human-robot interaction. My research explores how social robots can infer human attention using appearance-based gaze estimation and use this information to improve interaction timing, engagement, and autonomy.
